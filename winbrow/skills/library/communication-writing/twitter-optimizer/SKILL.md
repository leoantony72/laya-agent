# Twitter / X Optimizer

## When to Use

Use when GOAL asks to optimize, rewrite, or critique a tweet, thread, or post
for reach and engagement.

## When NOT to Use

- Do not use to fabricate news, impersonate someone, or evade platform rules.
- Do not use for mass automation, follow/unfollow schemes, or spam.
- Do not promise a specific number of impressions or a viral outcome.
- Do not add hashtags that are unrelated to the content.

## Review Dimensions

1. **Hook** — the first line must earn the click in under two seconds. Prefer a
   concrete claim, tension, or a specific result.
2. **Clarity** — one idea per post. Remove throat-clearing and jargon.
3. **Retention** — short lines, concrete nouns, and a reason to keep reading.
4. **Reply prompt** — ask a question that is easy and interesting to answer.
5. **Credibility** — numbers, sources, or a personal receipt; no vague claims.
6. **Formatting** — line breaks for scanability, no walls of text.
7. **Risk** — check for misleading claims, dog whistles, and accidental
   ambiguity.

## Step Pattern

1. Identify the goal: reach, replies, clicks, follows, or a specific action.
2. Diagnose the draft against the seven dimensions. Quote the weak line.
3. Rewrite in 2–3 variants:
   - direct and specific
   - contrarian or curiosity-driven (only if honest)
   - story/receipt-led
4. Keep the user's voice and facts. Do not invent a result.
5. Explain the tradeoff of each variant in one line.
6. Offer a thread version only if the idea genuinely needs more than one post.
7. End with a posting checklist: claim checked, link works, no accidental
   ambiguity.

## Output Shape

A diagnosis table, 2–3 rewritten variants, and a short "why this may perform
better" note.

## Recovery

- Draft is already strong: say so and make only line-level edits.
- Claim is unverifiable: flag it and offer a version without the claim.
- User asks for engagement bait: explain the reputational risk and offer an
  honest alternative.
